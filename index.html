Dr Cathy O'Brien

I explore how digital platforms govern participation and the consequences this has for who is able to build community, belong, and stay safe in digital spaces. My work sits at the intersection of digital anthropology, human-computer interaction, and platform governance, and is grounded in qualitative and digital ethnographic methods.

Overview

Research

I am a Senior Research Fellow in Human-Computer Interaction at the UCL Department of Computer Science.

I completed my DPhil in Cognitive and Anthropological Archaeology at the University of Oxford, funded by the Clarendon Fund. My doctoral research used digital sensory ethnography to examine how people learned pottery skills through digital media during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the impacts this had on their wellbeing.

I then joined the Information Security Group at UCL, working on the digital forensic triage in policing project, before moving into online safety and platform governance research through the UseCRT project and beyond.

Current & Recent

Projects

Google Gift Grant · UCL

Reimagining Privacy Harm Reporting Using Conversational User Interfaces

Exploring how conversational AI and large language models might support the reporting of complex privacy harms, through interviews and co-design workshops with practitioners in policing, counselling, and restorative justice.

Google Award · KCL

Scammed by Love: AI-Facilitated Romance Fraud

Cross-national study of AI-facilitated romance scams targeting older adults, combining focus groups, a survey, and community workshops. Collaborating with George Mason University.

REPHRAIN · UCL

UseCRT: Usability of Online Content Reporting Tools

Systematic analysis of 517 policy-related documents across 25 platforms, examining how platforms construct and govern harassment.

UKRI · UCL

Digital Forensic Triage in Policing

Examining how frontline officers triage digital evidence at scene, using co-design and ethnographic methods.

Clarendon Fund · University of Oxford ·

Potting in the Pandemic: Pottery Skill Development and its Wellbeing impact in Covid-19 Britain

Digital sensory ethnographic study of how people used digital media alongside the tools and materials of pottery to learn craft skills during the pandemic, and the positive and negative wellbeing impacts of this. .

Installation · 2023

Café Culture

Missing Bean Café & School of Archaeology, University of Oxford. A year-long research and art installation, made in collaboration with members of the School of Archaeology, communicating cognitive archaeology and anthropological research about pottery to a general audience.

Podcast · 2023

OxPods Podcast: Pottery in the Pandemic

Interviewed for the Oxford student podcast OxPods on my DPhil research into pottery skill learning and wellbeing during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Research Associate · 2022

Talking Memory Project

Ashmolean Museum, University of Oxford. Selected as one of five individuals to participate in this nine-month project exploring memory and how museums can support wellbeing in older adults. Developed and delivered a public engagement event integrating object-based research, interdisciplinary theory, and participatory art activities to promote social connection and reflective memory work in museum settings. Pre- and post-session measures showed that participants felt the experience had positively impacted their in-the-moment wellbeing.
